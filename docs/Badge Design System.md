# Badge Design System

## Purpose

This document is the canonical visual standard for all Melanated Adventurers achievement badges.

When a product request says **create a badge**, **add a badge**, **make a new badge**, or otherwise introduces a collectible achievement badge, this specification applies automatically. The badge should be designed as a member of the existing badge family without requiring a new style discussion.

The repository, not chat history, is the source of truth for this standard.

## Canonical Badge Family

The established badge family includes examples such as:

- Camp Crew
- Water Wayfinder
- Summit Seeker
- Trailblazer
- Star Gazer
- Community Builder
- Legacy

New badges must look as though they were produced in the same physical patch collection.

## Core Visual Language

### 1. Embroidered patch, never generic iconography

Badges must have the appearance of a premium embroidered outdoor patch that could physically be sewn onto a backpack, jacket, camp blanket, or gear bag.

Do not substitute:

- flat vector icons
- generic medal icons
- simple line art
- app-glyph style symbols
- glossy mobile-game achievement coins
- minimalist geometric badges

The artwork should visibly suggest thread, stitching, layered embroidery, and tactile fabric depth.

### 2. Dark navy foundation

The patch foundation is deep navy / midnight blue. The badge should retain strong contrast against the app's dark interface while still reading as a physical object rather than a UI tile.

### 3. Gold embroidered border

Every badge uses a substantial gold or golden-orange embroidered perimeter. The edge should feel stitched and dimensional, not like a thin digital stroke.

Gold is the visual connective tissue across the badge family.

### 4. Rich illustrated scene

Each badge contains a detailed illustrated scene that communicates the achievement at a glance.

The scene should be specific to the badge concept. Examples include:

- campfire and tents for community camping
- paddling and water for water activities
- a hiker at elevation for mountain achievement
- an off-road vehicle for trail exploration
- telescope and night sky for stargazing
- people together for community contribution
- a mature tree for legacy

For badges involving people, represent Black outdoor adventurers naturally and prominently where people are appropriate to the concept.

The image should feel narrative, not symbolic-only.

### 5. Integrated title banner

The badge name appears as part of the physical patch itself on an embroidered ribbon, banner, or integrated lower title area.

The title is typically uppercase and designed to feel woven or stitched into the badge.

The title should not be rendered as loose app-interface text floating inside the artwork.

### 6. Supporting emblem

Where composition allows, use a small supporting emblem near the bottom of the badge, such as a compass rose, campfire, wave, mountain, handshake, infinity symbol, star, or another badge-specific mark.

This is secondary to the main illustrated scene.

## Shape System

Badge silhouettes may vary to keep the collection visually interesting. Approved families include:

- rounded arch / shield
- pointed mountain crest
- circular crest
- hexagonal or structured shield
- specialty crest for rare or legacy achievements

Variation is encouraged, but the following must remain consistent:

- substantial embroidered outline
- dark navy structural base
- gold stitched edge treatment
- illustrated center scene
- integrated title banner
- comparable visual density and dimensionality

A new shape must still look unmistakably related to the existing collection.

## Color System

The badge family uses a rich outdoor palette anchored by:

- deep navy / midnight blue
- warm gold / golden orange stitching
- forest and pine greens
- mountain and water blues
- sunset oranges and reds
- natural earth browns
- cream / warm white highlights

Category-specific accent colors may be used without replacing the shared navy-and-gold foundation.

Examples:

- water achievements can emphasize blues and aquas
- community achievements may add purple accents
- special / legacy achievements may add deep red or magenta accents
- skill achievements may add cyan accents

## Texture and Rendering

Artwork should be:

- highly detailed at badge scale
- dimensional
- thread-textured
- richly shaded
- clean enough to remain readable when reduced in the mobile UI
- visually consistent with premium embroidered souvenir patches

Avoid photorealism. The target is illustrated embroidery, not a photograph printed onto fabric.

## Composition Requirements

Each badge should contain, in order of importance:

1. Main illustrated achievement scene
2. Badge title integrated into the patch
3. Strong gold embroidered silhouette / edge
4. Optional secondary emblem
5. Supporting environmental detail

The central scene must remain readable when the badge is shown in a compact profile card.

## Artwork vs. App UI

The badge image itself should include only elements that belong to the physical collectible.

### Belongs inside badge artwork

- illustrated scene
- embroidered title / ribbon
- stitched decorative details
- supporting emblem

### Does not belong inside badge artwork

- category pills such as `ACTIVITY`, `SKILLS`, `COMMUNITY`, or `SPECIAL`
- earned date
- the word `Achievement` as UI metadata
- card borders or card background
- buttons
- progress indicators
- app navigation

Those elements belong to the surrounding product interface.

## Accessibility and Small-Size Readability

Badges must be evaluated at the actual mobile display size, not only as large generated artwork.

At small size:

- the silhouette must remain recognizable
- the primary subject must still be understandable
- the title banner must not collapse into visual noise
- important faces or objects should not rely on tiny details
- gold edging should maintain separation from dark UI backgrounds

## New Badge Workflow

Whenever a new badge is requested:

1. Determine what accomplishment the badge represents.
2. Select one clear visual story for that accomplishment.
3. Choose an appropriate silhouette from the approved shape family.
4. Build the scene using the canonical navy, gold, and outdoor palette.
5. Place the achievement name in an integrated embroidered banner.
6. Add a supporting emblem when useful.
7. Verify the result visually belongs beside Camp Crew, Water Wayfinder, Summit Seeker, Trailblazer, Star Gazer, Community Builder, and Legacy.
8. Verify readability at the actual app-card size.
9. Add the final production asset to the badge asset catalog and map it explicitly to the badge code. Do not rely on generic fallback artwork for a production badge.

## Generation Brief Template

Use the following as the base creative brief for any newly generated badge artwork:

> Create a premium embroidered outdoor achievement patch for Melanated Adventurers named `[BADGE NAME]`. It must belong to the same visual family as Camp Crew, Water Wayfinder, Summit Seeker, Trailblazer, Star Gazer, Community Builder, and Legacy. Use a dark navy fabric foundation, thick dimensional gold embroidered edging, rich thread texture, and a detailed illustrated outdoor scene representing `[ACHIEVEMENT CONCEPT]`. Integrate `[BADGE NAME]` into an embroidered ribbon/banner across the lower portion of the patch. Use rich forest, mountain, water, sunset, and earth tones appropriate to the concept. Include Black outdoor adventurers naturally when people belong in the scene. The result should look like a collectible physical patch someone could sew onto outdoor gear, not a flat app icon, vector symbol, medal, or generic game achievement. Do not include category pills, dates, UI labels, cards, or interface elements in the artwork.

The brief should be adapted to the specific accomplishment, but its visual-system requirements are mandatory.

## Trailhead Badge Direction

The Trailhead joining badge must use this same system.

Its visual story should communicate the beginning of a member's journey. Appropriate imagery includes a clearly marked trail entrance, trail marker, winding path, forest and/or mountains opening ahead, and a subtle compass or directional element.

It should feel like the first patch in the collection, not a generic medal or onboarding icon.

## Consistency Rule

If a proposed badge could not sit next to the established badge family without looking like it came from another brand or another product, it is not finished.

When there is uncertainty, consistency with the established physical embroidered-patch collection takes priority over novelty.
